# Smallest RSTAB test models for the 1.7 % sway difference and the column lean-direction offset (Phase 2, item 7)

Status: **for your review. Nothing in the application was changed.** The RSTAB columns of every table are empty: they have to come from RSTAB. The closed-form and solver values are in `benchmarks/rstab-test-models/reference-values.md` (generated by `npx tsx scripts/rstab/test-models.ts`).

## 1. What is known, and the question

* RSTAB Detail 2 / 3 against this solver (2,924 members, second order, shear on): support forces ≤ 0.1 %, displacements ≤ 1.7 %, member forces ≤ 2 %. **Sway is uniformly 1.7 % lower in this solver** (24.71 mm vs RSTAB's value; the print resolution of RSTAB displacements is 0.1 mm). Column moments in the lean direction show a near-constant offset of **at most 4.8 kNcm along the member** (shear identical). `docs/RSTAB_BENCHMARK.md` section 5.2 lists the hypotheses already rejected: element mesh, torsional geometric term, von Kármán, shear areas, imperfection magnitude, load distribution, gravity combination, stiffness factors.
* **Hypothesis H0 (one cause, not two):** the moment offset is the P-Δ moment of the sway difference. 1.7 % of 24.71 mm is 0.42 mm; with a column axial force N the corresponding moment is N × 0.42 mm: N = 114 kN gives 4.8 kNcm. The columns of the benchmark carry roughly 70 to 300 kN (RSTAB report: N = −67 to −300 kN at the extremes), so **the order of magnitude of the offset is what a 0.4 mm sway difference alone would produce**. Both observations may therefore be one effect (a slightly different P-Δ stiffness or geometry in RSTAB), not two. This is arithmetic, not a proof; the test models below decide it.

## 6. How the results settle the hypotheses

| RSTAB shows | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| TM1 second order equals the closed form (and the solver) | the P-Δ formulation is the same; the benchmark difference is **not** in the basic column formulation: look at the structure (braces, hinges, loads) and at the load definition (TM2, TM4) |
| TM1 differs from the closed form by about 1.7 % in sway | RSTAB's P-Δ column stiffness or geometry differs from the textbook solution by that amount: **root cause found**, and H0 explains the moment offset (N × Δ); then the question becomes whether this solver or RSTAB follows the second-order elastic analysis of Annex B (the closed form decides) |
| TM1 matches but TM2 differs | the point or distribution of RSTAB's equivalent imperfection loads differs from φ0·N at the top (hypothesis B2) |
| TM3 shows no amplification with 1 division and the closed form with 8 | RSTAB's P-Δ analysis needs member divisions for P-δ; the benchmark columns are divided into 2 members (4 000 mm and 500 mm): the P-δ capture of RSTAB's mesh could differ from this solver's 4 elements per column |
| TM4 differs in the diagonal state or the sway | tension-only treatment (hypothesis B3), e.g. RSTAB deactivating a brace this solver keeps active, or a different iteration state |
| TM5 differs between planes in RSTAB | axis orientation / β handling in RSTAB (hypothesis B1) |
| all five agree | the 1.7 % is a property of the large structure (a tolerance accumulation, the model reproduction of hinges or loads), to be searched in the full model with experiments A1-A2, B2-B6 of `OUTSTANDING_ITEMS_REGISTER.md` OI-07 |

No change of the implementation is proposed before these results (root cause and evidence first).

## 7. Closed-form sources and basis (for your review)
* Beam-columns: S. P. Timoshenko and J. M. Gere, *Theory of Elastic Stability*, 2nd ed., McGraw-Hill, 1961, chapter 1 (beam-columns with lateral loads); W. F. Chen and E. M. Lui, *Structural Stability: Theory and Implementation*, Elsevier, 1987. Formulas used: cantilever with axial P and tip load H, k = √(P/EI): u = (H/(P k))(tan kL − kL), M_base = (H/k) tan kL; simply supported beam with UDL: M_max = (q/k²)(sec(kL/2) − 1). **I quoted these from the standard derivations; please check them against your copy.** The derivation of the cantilever case: the beam-column equation EI y'' + P y = H (L − x) with y(0) = y'(0) = 0 gives the above.
* Portal (TM5): slope-deflection with antisymmetric sway, equal columns, joint rotations equal, beam axially rigid and shear-rigid: u = H h³(2 + 3k)/(12 E I_c (1 + 6k)), k = I_b h/(I_c L). Limit k → ∞ gives H h³/(24 E I_c), the sway of two fixed-fixed columns. Derivation in the script header comments is not given; it is the standard slope-deflection result and can be re-derived in a few lines.
* Limits: Euler-Bernoulli (no shear) in the closed forms; the solver "no shear" values use the same assumptions; the solver "shear" values include the shear areas of the RSTAB section.

## 8. Findings from preparing these models (defects of the first-order analysis; **fixed with your approval, regression-tested**)
These concern the **first-order** analysis only; the second-order results of this document (TM1, TM3, TM5) match the closed forms, and the benchmark comparison uses second order.

* **F-1 First-order station moments contain P-δ terms.** `recoverElements` (`src/server/frame/solve.ts`) recovers the interior section forces by equilibrium on the *deformed* geometry (the term `(ut − vx)·N`) in every analysis. In a first-order analysis with an axial force (TM3 with 651 kN) the printed mid-span moment is 14.1 kNm (1 or 2 elements) instead of 10.0 kNm (q L²/8), converging to 10.3 kNm at 8 elements. A first-order moment must be the linear one. Impact: first-order interior moments of members with axial force (end moments are unaffected). Design on first-order results is refused by the application, so no design result is affected; the display of first-order internal forces and any record built from them are. Proposed fix: pass the analysis order to `recoverElements` and omit the deformation terms when the analysis is first order; test with TM3 (first-order M = 10.000 kNm for any number of elements ≥ 2).
* **F-2 First-order analysis with tension-only members does not converge when a member switches off.** In first order the stiffness is assembled once and kept factorised (`evaluate(u, so, true)` with `so = false`), so when an N-only member becomes inactive the tangent is not updated; the iteration creeps and stops at the iteration limit. TM4 cases B and D (first order, diagonal compressed): "NOT converged: iteration limit of 100 reached". The same cases in second order converge (−50.5 mm, amplification 1.66 of the −30.3 mm first-order value). Proposed fix: re-assemble and re-factor the stiffness when the state of a tension-only element changes; test with TM4 B (hand result 30.32 mm for the diagonal off) and D. Impact: first-order results of models with N-only members under loading that compresses them; the benchmark first-order case (Detail 1, CO1) converged, so it does not trigger the defect.

**Fix applied (F-1 and F-2, approved), `src/server/frame/solve.ts`:** (F-1) the P-δ term `(ut − vx)·N` in the interior station moments is used only when the analysis is second order; (F-2) in first order the stiffness is re-assembled and re-factored every iteration when the model contains an active tension-only truss option, so the switching state is followed. Second-order results are unchanged. **Evidence** (`npm run test:frame`, 79 checks, 11 new): TM3 first-order mid-span moment = qL²/8 to 1e-9 for 2/4/8 elements; second-order 8 elements vs sec closed form 1.7e-3 % (unchanged); first-order cantilever moment diagram linear to 1e-8 Nmm; braced bay with tension-only diagonal converges when the diagonal is off and when active, carries no force when off, sway = H/(2·3EI/h³) (0.1 %), first order = second order at 1 N gravity, active diagonal ≥20× stiffer. Regression suites unchanged: test:rstab (all bounds met), integration 32, codes 99, design 115, legacy 51, restraint 48, engine, no-en, tsc clean.
